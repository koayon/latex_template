# Writing Intros

The intro serves as both a summary of the work and as a gate for
readers to decide whether they would like to read the rest of your paper.
You want to provide the narratve in both the text and in the
hook figure.
You also want to present your main contributions both in
the text and in the Contributions list. 

I like to think of the introduction as providing
a single page description of the narrative of the paper
as you would explain your work to a smart friend at a
whiteboard. 
Where possible using a key example in the intro will
make any abstract arguments seem more grounded.

You should make your key claims here allowing the body
of the paper to provide the detailed evidence to
support these claims. 

Introductions often contain paragraphs answering the following questions:
- What is the problem?
- Why is the problem interesting and important (and to whom)?
- Why is the problem hard to solve? Does the naive approach trivially work?
- Why hasn't this problem been solved before? How do we reframe the problem to solve it better?
- What are the key components of your solution? Explain these in a figure.
